Nikola Tesla

Engineer, inventor, and one of the fathers of alternating-current power

About Nikola Tesla

Nikola Tesla (1856-1943) was a Serbian-American inventor and electrical engineer whose work laid the foundation for the modern alternating-current power system. He filed hundreds of patents across electrical engineering, wireless transmission, and mechanical devices. Though he died with little money, his ideas quietly power almost every home today.

Key events

  1. 1856 — Born in Smiljan, in the Austrian Empire (modern-day Croatia).
  2. 1884 — Emigrated to the United States and briefly worked for Thomas Edison.
  3. 1888 — Sold his AC induction motor and polyphase patents to George Westinghouse.
  4. 1893 — AC power lit the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago.
  5. 1895 — Helped design the first AC hydroelectric plant at Niagara Falls.
  6. 1943 — Died in New York City, having filed close to 300 patents worldwide.

Key inventions & contributions

  • The AC induction motor and polyphase power system
  • The Tesla coil, a high-voltage transformer circuit
  • Early experiments in wireless power and radio transmission
  • Contributions to remote control and robotics concepts
  • Design work behind the first large-scale AC hydroelectric plant

Tesla's rivalry with Edison over AC versus DC power — later nicknamed the "War of the Currents" — is one of the most famous disputes in the history of technology, and AC ultimately became the world standard.
